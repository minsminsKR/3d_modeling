# Earned recognition impact and pursuit pressure

The requested horror direction uses a brief red peripheral shock, native pixel
grain and a short recorded impact at actual discovery, followed by rhythmic
recorded strings/percussion while a witnessed actor genuinely pursues the
player. The inspiration is the escape pressure described in the developer's
[Shadow Corridor 2 store page](https://store.steampowered.com/app/2310700/Shadow_Corridor_2/?l=koreana).
No asset, soundtrack, melody or sound from that game is copied.

`DetectionFeedback` retains its existing actual-sight admission, witnessed
identity registry, .8s recognition window and 4s impact cooldown. In normal
mode its sustained peripheral range is .56–.74 and native grain .59–.68. The
centre of the fixed alpha veil stays transparent, the existing HUD renders
normally and the player retains movement/look control. The player's camera
rotation, FOV and navigation/attack policy are unchanged. Reduced motion keeps
zero lens/chromatic/grain, a fixed quiet tint and the existing softer veil.

`DetectionPursuitAudio` adds two owned recognition variants and one 150 BPM,
6.4s repeating recorded pursuit bed. Its authority is the perception owner's
earned recognition counter and witnessed pressure, never an independent enemy
position scan. Unknown active Chase states therefore cannot reveal a hidden
enemy. Music rises and fades, obeys listener/source mute and pause, and stops
on disengagement, disable or owner cleanup. Existing physical monster sounds
keep their spatial role. Safe hiding ends the original chase through the
existing brain policy; actual sight after leaving can admit exactly one new
recognition instead of treating a fresh chase as the old episode.

Prepared WAVs, Unity metadata, the deterministic editing generator, candidate
selection, creators' pages, retained independent acoustic violin preview
recordings and licenses are in `SourceArt/Audio` and
`ThirdParty/Audio/DetectionPursuit`. The raw retained assets are documented MP3
previews, not mislabeled lossless originals. Inventory, hashes, reproducible
prepared bytes and loop-boundary checks are separate from gameplay tests.

Evidence covers four targeted Unity PlayMode cases, a native production-camera
and actual UI Toolkit render at calm/discovery/chase/comfort/escape, a natural
native full-mix capture, and an isolated new-voice lifecycle/mute assay. The
audio assay uses controlled supported school player/Cyclops poses and zero
actor speeds, actual opaque cover/LOS and a valid unknown Chase restore.
Its hiding fixture calls the public player Hide at a supported floor with a
local marker, rather than certifying authored cabinet geometry or survival.
The Mask context separately exercises actual fast navigation, ordinary door
destruction and authored cabinet interaction with the new production mix.

All automatic players use `-quiet-diagnostics`: current-process device-session
mute is verified before positive Unity DSP capture, while other game processes
are excluded. The final visual assay also explicitly waits for this handshake.
On exit listener gain becomes zero before original session mute and seven user
preferences are restored. Diagnostic WAVs and verbose logs remain optional
local evidence outside Git; reports retain their sample statistics and hashes.
These tests do not certify human listening, subjective fear, a naturally played
survival route or frame rate. Ordinary game launches keep the user's settings.
